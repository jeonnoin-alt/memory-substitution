### proposal: self_pool_break_even
Title: Break-Even Budgets: How Little Self-Collection a Reader Needs Before a Foreign Experience Pool Stops Paying
Short Hypothesis: The advantage of a foreign experience pool (expert walkthroughs or another backbone's successes) over a reader's own success-filtered pool is confined to a coverage-limited regime: k=3 retrieval saturates once each task type has about three same-type successful items in the bank, so a reader with per-type k=0 success p_t and type frequency f_t needs only about m/(p_t f_t) attempted collection episodes on type t to catch up. For Qwen3-32B on ALFWorld this is roughly 10% of the full 3,553-game budget (about 13 GPU-minutes); the expert pool's retained-gain ratio against the full self pool is at most 1.3; and a second backbone's pool substitutes for own collection within noise. The amortized value of a shared foreign pool is therefore a collection-cost saving, not a quality gain, except on success-starved types.

Experiments:
Preparation (CPU env replay, hours): render the 1,465 expert walkthroughs and the 1,892 Qwen3 successes into one item format by replaying actions in ALFWorld and recording observations; success-filter. Collection (GPU): verify EXAONE-4.0-32B in vLLM 0.13; collect its k=0 pool on the same 3,553 train games (about 3 GPU-hours) on one A100 while the other serves Qwen3 reader cells. Reader cells (each 274 validation games x 2 seeds = 548 paired episodes, about 30 min per replica): (A) anchors for the Qwen3 reader: k=0, static-procedure prompt (Measurement C), token-matched wrong-type placebo, expert full (Measurement A), self 100%; (B) self-pool budget sweep b in {0.5, 2.5, 10, 25}% x 2 draws, k=3 (8 cells) plus 100% (1); (C) expert subsets on the same game indices at the four sub-100% budgets (4 cells); (D) coverage arm m in {1, 3, 10} same-type items per type at full budget, for the self and expert banks (6 cells); (E) k=1 replicates: self 100%, self 10%, expert full (3 cells); (F) EXAONE reader: k=0, static, own 100%, own 10%, Qwen3 100%, Qwen3 10%, expert 100% (7 cells); Qwen3 reader on the EXAONE pool at 100% and 10% (2 cells). About 36 cells, roughly 19,800 episodes, about 9 wall-hours with one replica per GPU plus the 3 GPU-hour collection, scheduled so the collection job overlaps the Qwen3 reader cells; total about 1.5 days. Outputs per cell: success (aggregate, per type, seen/unseen), net vs k=0 and vs static prompt, injected tokens, steps; per writer x reader: retained-gain ratio at each budget, B*, per-type B*_t; amortized cost per net point for R readers sharing one pool vs R private pools.

Baselines and Ablations:
k=0; static per-type procedure prompt at matched tokens (Measurement C; every gain reported net of it); token-matched wrong-type placebo (same injected tokens, items from other types) to bound the value of any memory; k=1 vs k=3; format ablation: raw on-policy self items (with the writer's reasoning text) vs the replayed action-observation rendering, to show the rendering does not carry the effect; two independent draws at every sub-100% budget to separate subset variance from the budget effect; coverage caps m in {1,3,10} at full budget as the direct test of the saturation mechanism; expert-pool subsets at the same game indices as the matched-item control for the budget axis; writer swap in both directions (Qwen3 reads EXAONE's pool, EXAONE reads Qwen3's pool) at 100% and 10%; the Measurement A expert cell reused as the positive control that the pipeline reproduces +27 net.

Falsifiable Predictions:
P1 (headline, saturation): for the Qwen3-32B reader at k=3, the self pool collected at b=10% lands within 8 net points of the self pool at 100% in both draws. Falsifier: either 10% draw is at least 8 net below the full self pool with the paired CI excluding 0; arm: self-10% vs self-100% cells. P2 (coverage): capping the full bank at m=3 same-type items per type stays within 8 net of m=all, for both the self and the expert bank. Falsifier: m=3 is at least 8 net below m=all in either bank; arm: coverage cells. Positive control for P1-P2: b=0.5% (types with zero same-type items) and m=1 must fall at least 8 net below full; if they do not, the sweep is insensitive and P1-P2 are declared uninformative rather than confirmed. P3 (expert vs self at full budget): the expert pool's gain minus the full self pool's gain is below 8 net with CI including 0 (retained-gain ratio expert/self at most 1.3). Falsifier: expert minus self at least 8 with CI excluding 0; arm: expert-full vs self-100% at k=3 (the reverse outcome, self beating expert by 8, is also reported and would falsify the ratio bound from the other side). P4 (per-type ordering): at b=2.5% the per-type deficit of the self pool relative to its 100% version is largest for heat and smallest for pick-and-place, with Spearman rho between the per-type deficit and 1/(p_t f_t) at least 0.6 pooled over both draws, both readers and both k. Falsifier: rho at most 0.2, or heat not among the two largest deficits; arm: per-type deficits from the b=2.5% cells. P5 (writer swap): for each reader, the other backbone's full pool is within 8 net of the reader's own full pool. Falsifier: own minus other at least 8 with CI excluding 0 in either reader; arm: writer-swap cells. None of P1-P5 is entailed: retrieval could require many near-duplicate items (falsifying P1-P2), on-policy successes could carry inefficient or lucky procedures (falsifying P3 either way), per-type deficits could be flat (P4), and an own pool could be privileged by proximity (P5).

Measurement and Noise Control:
All cells are paired on the same 274 validation games with the same two seeds per game and the same retrieval ordering; the resampling unit for bootstrap CIs is the game (seeds nested), stratified by the six task types. Primary statistics are paired net-success differences; retained-gain ratios are reported with joint paired-bootstrap CIs and only when the denominator's CI excludes 0 (otherwise the difference is the estimand, since the ratio is ill-conditioned). The noise floor of a 274 x 2 cell is about +/-8 net; all margins are set at 8 net, which is 30% of the +27 Measurement A effect; the Gate for the positive control is the same 8. Order of runs: k=0 and static prompt first, then the two anchors (expert full, self 100%), then the sweep; the sweep is stopped early if P3 falsifies with expert beating self by more than 16, since then break-even is not reached within budget and the reported quantity becomes the residual expert advantage per type. Subset variance is estimated from the two draws per budget and reported alongside the budget effect. Per-type analyses pool across readers, draws and k to reach about 45 games x 2 seeds x 8 cells per type; per-type CIs are reported and the coverage arm (full-cell power) is the confirmatory test of the mechanism. Throughput and vLLM queue occupancy are logged per cell; both A100s stay busy by overlapping the EXAONE collection with Qwen3 reader cells.

### proposal: reader_pays_writer_detours
Title: The Reader Pays the Writer's Detours: Consumer-Side Cost of Transferred Experience Tracks the Writer's Episode Length, Not Its Success
Short Hypothesis: The consumer-side cost of using a foreign experience pool (generated tokens, observation tokens and steps per episode) is inherited from the writer's trajectory length: a reader copies the number of steps of the retrieved item, so on-policy self pools (long, with detours) cost more per successful episode than minimal expert walkthroughs at equal success. Pruning replay-verified detours from self items at fixed content removes the inherited cost without losing success, and padding expert walkthroughs with benign detours adds it; consequently the ranking of writers by gain per consumer token differs from their ranking by success, and cost-matched comparisons across writers must charge the reader's generated tokens, not only the injected ones.

Experiments:
Preparation (CPU env replay): render all pools into one item format; build self-pruned and expert-padded variants with replay verification (drop and count items that fail replay); log item lengths (mean steps: expert, self-raw, self-pruned, expert-padded, EXAONE-raw, EXAONE-pruned). Collection: EXAONE k=0 pool on the 3,553 train games (about 3 GPU-hours) on one A100 while the other serves Qwen3 reader cells. Reader cells (274 games x 2 seeds, about 30 min per replica): Qwen3 reader at k=3: expert (replayed), expert-padded, self-raw, self-pruned, EXAONE-raw, EXAONE-pruned, k=0, static-procedure prompt, token-matched wrong-type placebo (9 cells); Qwen3 at k=1: expert, self-raw, self-pruned (3 cells); EXAONE reader at k=3: expert, expert-padded, Qwen3-raw, Qwen3-pruned, own-raw, own-pruned, k=0, static (8 cells). About 20 cells, roughly 11,000 episodes, about 5 wall-hours on two replicas plus the collection; about 1 day. Per episode we log success, steps, generated tokens, observation tokens, injected tokens, the top-1 retrieved item's id and length, and whether the step cap was hit. Analyses: (i) paired net success per cell; (ii) reader steps and consumer tokens in successful episodes (and in all episodes with censoring at the step cap reported); (iii) the length-inheritance slope estimated on the manipulation pairs (padded vs expert; raw vs pruned) by regressing the paired within-game change in reader steps on the change in top-1 item steps, per reader; (iv) gain per 1k consumer tokens and the amortized cost per net point per writer x reader.

Baselines and Ablations:
k=0; static-procedure prompt at matched tokens (Measurement C); token-matched wrong-type placebo; k=1 vs k=3; raw on-policy rendering (with the writer's reasoning text) vs replayed action-observation rendering for the self pool, to show format does not carry the length effect; pruning-rule ablation: rule-based pruning vs random deletion of the same number of steps (replay-verified where possible) to show the cost effect is about length, not about which steps are removed; padding ablation: detours sampled from the reader's own failures vs from the other backbone's failures; the known k=3 vs k=7 injected-token difference (430 vs 1,010) as the positive control that the token accounting resolves cost differences; cells with the manipulated item's tokens held equal to the unmanipulated version by truncating observation text (token-matched length control) to separate item steps from item tokens.

Falsifiable Predictions:
P1 (headline, length inheritance): in both manipulation pairs (expert vs expert-padded; self-raw vs self-pruned) for the Qwen3 reader at k=3, generated reader steps in successful episodes rise by at least 0.3 per added item step, with the 95% CI lower bound above 0.1. Falsifier: the slope's CI upper bound is at most 0.1 in both pairs, meaning the reader plans from the item rather than copying its path; arm: the four manipulation cells. Injected tokens rise with padding by construction and are excluded from this estimand. P2 (pruning is success-neutral): self-pruned is within 6 net of self-raw in both readers. Falsifier: pruned at least 6 net below raw with CI excluding 0, the outcome the cue-discarding account of arxiv:2605.24117 predicts; arm: pruned vs raw cells. P3 (padding is success-neutral): expert-padded is within 6 net of expert. Falsifier: padded at least 6 net below expert, meaning detours contaminate content rather than only cost; arm: padded vs expert cells. P4 (cost reranks writers): for the Qwen3 reader, gain per 1k consumer tokens of the expert pool exceeds that of self-raw by at least 25% while their success gains are within 8 net. Falsifier: self-raw's gain per consumer token is at least the expert's, or the success gap is at least 8 so that the cost ranking merely follows success; arm: expert vs self-raw cells with token accounting. P5 (foreign reader inherits): EXAONE reading Qwen3-raw vs Qwen3-pruned shows a slope of at least 0.3. Falsifier: slope CI upper bound at most 0.1 for the foreign reader while at least 0.3 for the self reader, meaning inheritance is a self-recognition effect; arm: EXAONE cells. None is entailed: the reader may ignore item structure (P1, P5), detours may carry object-location cues (P2), inserted detours may mislead (P3), and cost may simply track success (P4).

Measurement and Noise Control:
All cells are paired on the same 274 games and two seeds with the same retrieval ordering; bootstrap over games, stratified by type. Success margins: 6 net for the neutrality predictions (the Gate-0 harm threshold) and 8 for cross-writer differences (the cell noise floor). Steps and tokens are continuous per-episode measures with far lower noise than success: a 100-game pilot estimates their SD (expected about 6 steps for successful episodes) so that about 500 paired successful episodes per pair resolve a slope difference of 0.2 with power above 0.9; the slope is estimated on within-game paired differences so game difficulty cancels. Censoring at the step cap is reported and the slope is re-estimated with cap-hitting episodes excluded and included. Consumer tokens are counted from the vLLM usage fields per request and summed per episode; injected tokens are reported separately so the entailed part of any cost change is visible. Order: k=0, static prompt and the two unmanipulated writers first; manipulation cells only after the unmanipulated expert cell reproduces Measurement A within 8 net. Throughput and queue occupancy per replica are logged; the EXAONE collection overlaps the Qwen3 reader cells so both A100s stay busy.

### proposal: leave_one_type_out_split
Title: Within-Type Splits Inflate Transfer: A Leave-One-Type-Out Composite Shows Retrieval Gains Are Type-Bound, Harm Tracks Within-Type Gain, and Only Weights Carry Portable Competence
Short Hypothesis: The standard ALFWorld splits (valid_seen/valid_unseen) always leave same-type items in the bank, so every measured transfer gain is procedure retrieval. Under a leave-one-type-out (LOTO) composite, in which each validation game is read against a bank lacking its own task type, retrieval gains from every writer collapse to the noise floor, and the writer that helps most within-type (the off-policy expert, whose items the reader follows most closely) becomes the most harmful under type shift, while the reader's own pool is near neutral. Absorbing the same expert pool into QLoRA weights, evaluated with the matching LOTO adapter, retains a positive type-portable gain (navigation and action validity), so the retrieval-versus-weights ranking flips between splits at matched cost.

Experiments:
Preparation: render pools by env replay; construct six LOTO banks per writer (type excluded by the ALFWorld task-type label; pick-two and pick-and-place are recorded as a known skeleton-similar pair and analysed separately); build the wrong-procedure graft pool (each item's appliance step swapped to the wrong appliance, replay-checked to fail). Collection: EXAONE k=0 pool (about 3 GPU-hours). Training on one A100 while the other serves: seven QLoRA r32 adapters on the expert pool (one all-type, six LOTO, about 2 GPU-hours each, about 14 GPU-hours), then served via vLLM multi-LoRA on the bf16 base with per-request adapter selection. Reader cells (274 games x 2 seeds): Qwen3 reader at k=3: standard bank for self and EXAONE writers (expert from Measurement A) (2), LOTO banks for expert, self, EXAONE (3), random-other-type placebo (1), five-procedure static prompt (1), wrong-procedure graft positive control (1); adapter cells: all-type adapter memory-absent (1), LOTO adapter composite memory-absent (1), LOTO adapter + LOTO retrieval of the expert pool (matched) (1), all-type adapter + standard expert retrieval (matched) (1), LOTO adapter + LOTO self-pool retrieval (mismatched) (1); EXAONE reader at k=3: standard banks for expert, own, Qwen3 (3), LOTO banks for the same three (3), k=0, six- and five-procedure static prompts (3). About 22 cells, roughly 12,100 episodes, about 5.5 wall-hours on two replicas plus 14 GPU-hours of adapter training and 3 GPU-hours of collection overlapped on the second GPU; about 2 days. The LOTO self cell for the Qwen3 reader is run with 4 seeds to power the seen/unseen decomposition. Per cell: success, net vs k=0 and vs the appropriate static prompt, per type, seen/unseen, injected tokens, steps, and the cost per net point (collection GPU-hours, training GPU-hours, consumer tokens).

Baselines and Ablations:
k=0; six-procedure static prompt (Measurement C) for standard cells and five-procedure static prompt (target type omitted) for LOTO cells, both token-matched; random-other-type placebo to separate nearest-wrong-type retrieval from any wrong-type content; wrong-procedure graft at 100% as the harm positive control (Gate 0: at least 6 net harm required); adapters evaluated memory-absent, matched and mismatched per the standing rule; adapter trained on the self pool (one all-type adapter, 2 GPU-hours) as the writer ablation for the parametric arm if time permits; standard-bank cells reused from the sibling designs where identical; k=1 LOTO cells for expert and self as the retrieval-strength ablation; pick-two/pick-and-place leakage check by excluding both types together in a seventh bank.

Falsifiable Predictions:
P1 (headline, type-bound retrieval): every writer x reader LOTO retrieval cell at k=3 gains at most +8 net vs k=0 on valid_unseen (CI upper bound), while the same writer's standard-bank cell gains at least +16. Falsifier: any LOTO cell shows at least +12 net with CI excluding +8, meaning that writer's items carry type-portable knowledge; arm: LOTO cells for three writers x two readers. P2 (harm tracks within-type gain): the writer with the largest within-type gain (predicted: expert) shows LOTO harm of at least 6 net vs k=0 in at least one reader, and the writer ranking by within-type gain equals the ranking by LOTO harm in both readers (Kendall tau = 1 over three writers). Falsifier: expert LOTO at or above -2 net (no harm), or the two rankings disagree; arm: standard and LOTO cells for three writers x two readers. Gate 0: the wrong-procedure graft must produce at least 6 net harm; if it does not, the reader is insensitive to wrong items and P2 is void, not confirmed. P3 (substrate flip): the LOTO adapter composite gains at least +8 net vs the base model without retrieval and exceeds LOTO retrieval of the same expert pool by at least 8, while within-type the k=3 expert retrieval exceeds the all-type adapter by at least 8. Falsifier: LOTO adapter at most +4, or LOTO retrieval at or above the LOTO adapter, or the all-type adapter at or above within-type retrieval; arm: adapter cells vs retrieval cells. P4 (static-prompt survival): the LOTO adapter gain exceeds the five-procedure static prompt by at least 6 net. Falsifier: static-5 at or above adapter minus 4; arm: five-procedure static cell vs LOTO adapter composite. P5 (room-bound residual): for the self writer, LOTO gain on valid_seen exceeds LOTO gain on valid_unseen by at least 10 net (own exploration items reveal object locations in familiar rooms), measured with 4 seeds. Falsifier: seen minus unseen at most 2 under LOTO for the self writer; arm: the 4-seed LOTO self cell split by seen/unseen. None is entailed: other-type items could teach navigation (P1), self items could harm as much as expert items (P2), adapters could overfit to the trained types (P3), the five-procedure prompt could carry the adapter's generic gains (P4), and room cues may be absent from success-filtered items (P5).

Measurement and Noise Control:
All cells are paired on the same 274 games and seeds with the same retrieval ordering; game-level stratified bootstrap CIs; the LOTO composite keeps the full 274 x 2 cell so the +/-8 noise floor applies to every headline comparison, unlike per-type held-out evaluation (about 45 games per type, +/-20). Margins: 8 net for gains (30% of the Measurement A effect), 6 net for harm (Gate 0), and 10 net for the seen/unseen decomposition with the 4-seed cell (halved cell size). The type-bound fraction is reported as a paired difference (standard minus LOTO on the same games) with its CI. Rank agreement across writers (P2) is reported with a bootstrap over games. Adapter cost is charged in GPU-hours per reader and retrieval cost in injected tokens per episode, and both are converted to cost per net point under each split. Order: k=0, static prompts and the graft positive control first (Gate 0 must pass before any LOTO harm claim), then the retrieval LOTO cells, then the adapter cells as they finish training; adapter training runs on the second GPU throughout so no serving replica idles. Queue occupancy and throughput per replica are logged per cell.
