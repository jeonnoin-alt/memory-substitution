archive:provenance_gap_selection_not_authorship: Selection, Not Authorship: The Signed Writer–Reader Capability Gap in Agent Memory, and Why Prompt Optimization Hides It. Memory-item utility is governed by the *signed* capability gap between the policy that wrote the item and the policy that reads it — downward transfer (strong writer → weak reader) is harmful, upward transfer is not — and the cure is reader-in-the-loop **selection**, not reader **authorship**. Two sharp consequences follow. (a) A quality/utility dissociation: frontier-written items win blind quality judging while losing downstream utility for a small reader, at matched source tasks, item count and injected tokens. (b) A masking result for the track's central object: a GEPA-style prompt optimizer, whose instruction candidates are scored on the *reader's own* rollouts, is itself a reader-in-the-loop selector; coupling it to memory absorbs the provenance harm at the net-accuracy level while the break-count asymmetry persists underneath. That is why combined memory+optimizer papers report end-to-end wins they cannot attribute — the optimizer is silently paying for the memory's provenance debt. Agentic memory pipelines almost universally have a frontier model distill experience that a small agent then consumes.
