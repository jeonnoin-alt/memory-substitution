### proposal: aggregate_or_comply
Title: Aggregate or Comply: Compiled Playbooks, Whole-Bank Context, Fixed Exemplars and Top-k Retrieval Under a Manipulated Contamination Rate of the Experience Bank
Short Hypothesis: The four ways of consuming an experience bank differ in where items are combined: a per-query retriever exposes the agent to each item at its bank share, a fixed exemplar set exposes it to one realisation of that share, an LLM compiler aggregates before the agent sees anything, and a long-context reader aggregates (or not) at read time. Under a manipulated fraction p in {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1} of replay-validated wrong-procedure items, retrieval and fixed exemplars already lose at minority contamination (one wrong item in three is complied with, not outvoted), the LLM-compiled playbook stays flat at one-in-three and collapses at two-in-three, and the whole-bank arm decides whether an in-context reader is an aggregator or a complier. A wrong instruction in the playbook slot is followed at least as readily as wrong exemplars in the demonstration slot. The static route's first advantage over retrieval is robustness, not accuracy, and it is invisible in every existing comparison because their banks are clean.

Experiments:
Poison construction: for each expert heat/cool/clean trajectory, replace the procedure-defining action (e.g., heat with microwave) by a same-scene distractor sequence (e.g., put the object on stoveburner 1, take it back), leave all other steps intact, replay under the ALFWorld engine and keep only items with success = 0 and no invalid steps; contamination rate p in {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1} applied per procedure type by replacement (bank size and goal-sentence distribution unchanged; thirds match k=3 and the three-exemplar static sets, so exact compositions j = 3p wrong items in three exist for the fixed-sample arm). Every cell is 274 games x 2 seeds paired with the existing clean retrieval (A) and k=0 (C) cells unless stated; the procedure-type cell is ~137 games. Arms: R1 goal-query retrieval k=3 (A's retriever; p=0 exists at seeds 1-2 and is extended to seeds 3-4): p in {1/3, 2/3, 1}, the p=1/3 cell at 4 seeds; the realised number of poisoned items among the 3 retrieved is logged per episode. R2 LLM-compiled playbook: Qwen3-32B writes one procedure per type (all six, type-agnostic, ~400 tokens, cached) from 12 uniformly sampled same-type items with a compiler prompt that forbids knowledge outside the sample; three independent draws (sample and compilation) at p in {0, 1/3, 2/3}, one at p=1; each artifact audited per type as clean / poisoned / hedged, used only as a descriptive covariate, never as a gate. R3 rule-compiled majority playbook: majority extraction of the procedure-defining action template over the same 12-item samples, p in {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1} (majority clean w.p. ~0.93 at 1/3 and wrong w.p. ~0.93 at 2/3, so its step is by construction and is reported, not predicted). R4 whole-bank_80: 80 type-balanced items sampled from the contaminated bank (~40 procedure items, ~13 wrong at p=1/3), fixed order per seed, cached, realised contamination recorded, p in {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1}. R5 fixed exemplar set (type-conditioned static: three items of the goal's type, sampled once per draw): exact composition j wrong in three per procedure type, j in {0, 1, 2, 3}, with two draws at j=0, five at j=1, one each at j=2 and j=3. Exploratory if time remains: a realism cell where R1's p=1/3 contamination consists of stale items from a name-swap wrapper (microwave <-> stoveburner) so the wrong items were once right. Order: R1_p1 (P1 gate) first; then the decisive one-in-three cells R1_p1/3, R2_p1/3 x3, R4_p1/3, R5_j1 x5; then the static p=0 cells; then R6; then the remaining doses. Baselines and Ablations:
Baseline is the best static arm at each p (R2, R3, R5, and R6c at p=0), with retrieval reported as retrieval minus best static at matched tokens on the same (game, seed); the type-agnostic requirement is met by R2/R3/R4 (all six procedures, no type selection) and by the playbook half of R6; R5 is the type-conditioned static counterpart of R1. ### proposal: many_shot_averages_detours
Title: Reading the Whole Bank Averages Out Detours: Many-Shot Context Is Robust to Per-Item Noise Where Top-k Retrieval Commits to It
Short Hypothesis: When the bank holds the agent's own successful-but-detoured trajectories (self pool) instead of expert trajectories, top-k retrieval passes the detours of the items it commits to (the rank-1 item above all) into the episode (more steps, more step-limit failures, lower success), while the whole-bank route is insensitive to the detour rate because the shared procedure is the consensus across many noisy items and idiosyncratic detours have no consensus; the gap between routes grows with the bank's detour rate and with item count, and closes when detours (not merely tokens) are pruned from the items.

Experiments:
- Pools and banks. E: expert pool (1,465). S: self pool (1,892 successes). Per item: detour steps = steps beyond the expert solution length for the same game; detour visits = receptacle visits that did not yield the target object plus repeated identical actions; distributions reported by type and by expert-solution-length tercile. A: within each stratum (type x expert-solution-length tercile) the top tercile of S by detour steps, with stratum weights equal to S's, so A matches S on type and difficulty and differs only in detour steps. P: pruned S, detour visits deleted, the pruned action list replayed in the same game, only successful replays kept; P's coverage of S by stratum is reported. Sh: sham-pruned S, built from exactly P's source items and matched to P's token count per item (within 10 percent) by deleting state-neutral non-detour steps (look, inventory, examine, redundant go-to) and then shortening observation strings of non-detour steps, with every detour visit and its observation kept; replay-verified. Item tokens measured (E about 140; S expected about 2x; P about E; Sh equal to P).

Composition arms: M1 = the top S item at rank 1 followed by the top-2 E items; M2 = the top E item at rank 1 followed by the top-2 S items (same retriever, same games). WB: whole bank as a type-agnostic cached static prefix. Block design: the 274 games are split into 8 type-stratified blocks (about 34 games each); each block gets its own random type-balanced bank drawn from the pool, and S banks are drawn with a tilt (2 blocks from the bottom tercile of detour steps within type, 4 uniform, 2 from the top tercile) so the bank-mean detour spans the pool's range while the arm mean equals the pool mean; episodes run block by block so one prefix is hot at a time. Rungs: E at 6 and 48 items, S at 6, 24 and 48. Token-matched comparison WB E48 vs WB S24 also reported.

RET E is Measurement A; k0 and the static arms are Measurement C.
- Outcomes: success (primary), steps per episode and step-limit failures (secondary, paired). Mechanism regressions: in RET arms, the per-(game, seed) step difference against RET E regressed on the injected items' detour steps (rank-1 item and mean over 3); in WB48 S, the same difference against WB48 E regressed on the detour steps of the most-similar prefix item (by retriever score, varying per game) and on the block bank-mean detour steps (8 tilted values, block-level bootstrap). Exploratory consensus signature: whether the episode visits the type's modal wrong receptacle (the receptacle most often visited without yield across S items of that type) regressed on that receptacle's share among the block bank's items and on whether the most-similar item visits it.
- Reader: Qwen3-8B on RET E, RET S, WB48 E, WB48 S as an exploratory replication of the 2x2 (4 arms, about 1 h).

- Order: RET S, then the positive control RET A vs RET S (stop rule); pre-registration including the slope MDE from measured step and detour SDs; then WB48 E and WB48 S (the 2x2 with RET E, RET S); then static3-S; then the S ladder and WB E6; then P and Sh; then M1 and M2; then Qwen3-8B.

### proposal: binding_scope_leakage_ladder
Title: Global Fact, Scene Table, or Instance Replay: The Scope of Instance Bindings Sets How Much of a Memory Gain Is Near-Duplicate Leakage and Whether a Static Prompt Can Match It
Short Hypothesis: An experience bank helps on a new task instance only through information that transfers from bank items to that instance. The scope of the instance bindings (global = per object class, per scene, per instance) fixes both the smallest static substitute for retrieval and the share of the retrieval gain that is near-duplicate leakage: at class scope one sentence per class matches scene-query retrieval and one bank item per class suffices; at scene scope one static (scene, class) table matches it; at instance scope no static prefix smaller than one row per instance exists, the gain exists only through the instance's own duplicate, it is linear in the duplicate rate because duplicates neither spill over to non-duplicated instances nor get discounted when rare, and a whole-bank prefix that contains the duplicate cannot replace the query. The natural ALFWorld bank sits at class scope (unseen >= seen), so its measured +17.2 net residual over the best static arm (static3) compiles into about 20 class-location sentences.

Experiments:
1. Gate (pre-registered, the positive control shared with the relocation design): scene scope, s=1, relocated x 25-step: scene-query retrieval minus static3-relocated >= +14 net; if it fails the ladder is not run and the failure is reported. 2. Construction: three relocation rules applied to the PDDL init of the 274 eval games and of the train games in eval scenes; expert trajectories regenerated with the ALFWorld PDDL expert (CPU); instance-scope duplicates are the expert trajectories of the eval games' own relocated inits, placed in the bank for a fraction s of games (s=0.5 as two complementary seed-drawn halves A and B); static3-relocated is rebuilt per scope from the relocated bank with the same three exemplar slots as Measurement C; the compiled sentence and table are written by rule from the bank; eval games sharing (goal text, scene) with another eval game are counted and form the tied subset. 3. Natural-bank anchor (1 cell): class-location table static (one sentence per class listing the receptacles where that class was picked in the natural train bank, <= 430 tokens, cached), paired against Measurement C's k3 and static3 rows on the same (game, seed) under Measurement C settings (k3 re-run as one extra cell if the vLLM build changed). 4. Confirmatory cells, each 274 games x 2 seeds = 548 episodes: scene scope (6): retrieval s=1, retrieval s=0, static3-relocated, type-agnostic, table static, whole-bank; global scope (6): retrieval full bank, retrieval 20-item bank, static3-relocated, type-agnostic, compiled sentence, whole-bank-20; instance scope (7): retrieval s=1, s=0.5 draw A, s=0.5 draw B, s=0, static3-relocated, type-agnostic, whole-bank. 5. Exploratory (2 cells): on-policy self-duplicate at instance scope s=1 (the game's own verified success from the type-agnostic cell, which doubles as the first pass; solved games only) versus the expert duplicate; stale duplicate (the same game's canonical pre-relocation expert trajectory) at instance scope s=1. Logged without episodes: rank of the self-duplicate under scene-query and goal-query. 6. Arithmetic: 20 confirmatory + 2 exploratory (+1 optional k3 re-run) = 22-23 cells x 548 = 12.1k-12.6k episodes; at Measurement C's throughput (~19 episodes/min on two A100s, one vLLM replica per GPU) about 10.5-11 h, static cells faster because their prefixes are cached. 7. Cost: cached and uncached prefill tokens and wall-clock per episode; accuracy-cost curve per scope.

Baselines and Ablations:
Static (cached, byte-identical prefixes): static3-relocated (reference static arm at every scope; three fixed type-conditioned expert exemplars rebuilt from each scope's relocated bank, ~430 tokens); type-agnostic six-procedure prompt (no bindings; run at every scope, controls type conditioning); compiled sentence per class (global scope, ~20 sentences); compiled (scene, class) table as one static prefix (scene scope, <= 274 rows); class-location table (natural bank); whole-bank: the 20-item one-per-class bank in context at global scope, the scene-restricted bank at scene and instance scope (all bank items from the eval game's scene, <= 80 in fixed game-id order, the eval game's own duplicate included at instance scope s=1; banks over 80 items drop train items by highest game id first and counts are reported). Retrieval: scene-query k=3 (goal plus first observation), the Measurement C retriever; ablations: 20-item one-per-class bank versus the full relocated bank at global scope; s=0 at scene and instance scope (scope-matched duplicates deleted); s=0.5 in two complementary draws at instance scope; stale and on-policy duplicates at instance scope (exploratory). Per the rules every retrieval cell is reported minus static3-relocated and minus the best static arm of its scope (max over the static arms above by pooled success). Dropped from the previous version: the global s=0 cell (P3 carries the global leakage claim), the k=1 and k=7 sweeps, and the random-type length placebo (Measurement C's blind1 bounds the length effect at -3 net).

Falsifiable Predictions:
Margins follow the brief: the +-8 net floor at 274 x 2 is the smallest reportable difference and +14 is the confirmatory margin. P0 (natural-bank anchor, stated against Measurement C's k3 - static3 = +17.2 [+11.9, +22.4]) The class-location table static minus k3 >= -8 net on the natural bank, i.e. the table recovers at least +9 of the +17.2 over static3. Falsified by table - k3 < -8 in the anchor cell (the +17.2 is scene-family information a class sentence cannot carry; the natural bank then sits between class and scene scope). P1 (gate) At scene scope, s=1, retrieval minus static3-relocated >= +14 net. Falsified by < +8 in that cell; the ladder is then not run. P2 (class scope compiles to a sentence) Positive control read first: at global scope retrieval (full bank) minus static3-relocated >= +14, falsified by < +8 (the reader does not carry a class rule out of other-scene trajectories), in which case P2 and P3 are reported as not testable. P2 proper: compiled-sentence static minus retrieval >= -8 net. Falsified by sentence < retrieval - 8 in the sentence cell (the reader applies a concrete trajectory but not a stated rule). P3 (one item per class suffices) At global scope retrieval with the 20-item bank is within +-8 of retrieval with the full relocated bank. Falsified by 20-item < full - 8 in the 20-item cell (a many-shot per-class requirement). P4 (scene scope compiles to a table) Table static minus retrieval (s=1) >= -8 net at scene scope. Falsified by table < retrieval - 8 in the table cell (the reader cannot look up its scene's row among <= 274). P5 (instance scope: an exact duplicate is replayed) At instance scope, retrieval (s=1, expert self-duplicate present) minus static3-relocated >= +28 net. Falsified by < +20 (95% CI excluding +28) in that cell (the reader does not follow even its own instance's expert trajectory within 25 steps, or k=3 does not rank it first). P6 (headline: the instance-scope gain is the duplicate and nothing else) Read if residual(1) >= +14. At instance scope s=0.5, pooled over the two complementary draws so each term has n = 274 x 2: the residual on games whose duplicate is present, D(0.5), is >= residual(1) - 8, and the residual on games whose duplicate is absent, N(0.5), is <= residual(0) + 8; since the pooled residual at s equals s x D(s) + (1 - s) x N(s), this is the linear-in-s law with intercept residual(0). Falsified by D(0.5) <= residual(1) - 14 (discounting: the reader trusts a duplicate less when half of the bank's retrievals are non-duplicates; s=0.5 cells against the s=1 cell) or by N(0.5) >= residual(0) + 14 (spillover: other games' duplicates in the bank seed a search strategy that transfers to non-duplicated games; s=0.5 cells against the s=0 cell). residual(0) is a measured anchor, not a claim. P7 (the whole bank does not replace the query at instance scope) At instance scope s=1, the scene-restricted whole-bank arm (<= 80 items, the game's own duplicate among them) <= retrieval - 14 net. Falsified by whole-bank >= retrieval - 8 in that cell (the reader finds its own instance among 80 same-scene trajectories without a query). Shared terms: P5, P6 and P7 use the instance-scope s=1 retrieval cell, and P6 is read only when residual(1) >= +14; P1 and P4 share the scene-scope s=1 retrieval cell; P2 and P3 share the global full-bank cell.

Measurement and Noise Control:
Task success on identical (game, seed) pairs across arms within each scope; paired nets with a cluster bootstrap over games (1,000 resamples) and 95% CIs; the +-8 net floor at 274 x 2 is the minimum reportable margin, +14 the confirmatory margin (half the Gate-0 gain), and P5 uses +28 with its falsifier at +20 so that a 14-point discount in P6 is a reportable loss. The reference static arm is pre-registered as static3-relocated per scope; retrieval minus the best static arm of the scope is also reported for every cell. The s=0.5 draws are complementary halves of the 274 games, so D(0.5) and N(0.5) are each measured on every game once at each seed (n = 274 x 2, floor +-8) and do not depend on which games carry duplicates; the same expert duplicates are used in every instance-scope arm. Eval games tied on (goal text, scene) with another eval game are counted; the primary instance-scope analysis is on untied games and the tied subset is reported separately. Same decoding, vLLM build, prompt template and retriever as Measurement C; static prefixes byte-identical with cache hits verified from logs; cached and uncached prefill tokens and wall-clock per episode logged. Gate and P0-P7 pre-registered before the first confirmatory cell; per-type claims exploratory; valid_seen and valid_unseen reported separately and pooled.
