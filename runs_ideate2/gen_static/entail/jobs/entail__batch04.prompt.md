### proposal: state_key_pays_only_for_planted_bindings
Title: What the Query Can Index: Observation-Keyed Retrieval Beats Goal-Keyed Retrieval and the Best Static Prompt Only When the Bank Carries Scene-Keyed Bindings at a Controlled Rate
Short Hypothesis: The residual of experience retrieval over the best static prompt equals the within-type variance in the bank that the query key can index and a fixed prompt cannot. On ALFWorld the goal sentence already reveals type and object class, so a goal-keyed retriever cannot index anything a type-conditioned static prompt lacks, whereas an observation-keyed retriever can index scene identity. We plant scene-keyed bindings (where the target object is in this scene) into the bank for 0/25/50/100 percent of evaluation games and predict: every key's residual is inside the +/-8-net floor at 0 percent; the residual rises with the rate for the observation key only; a compact whole-binding table in a cached static prompt matches observation-keyed retrieval at 100 percent; and a wrong planted binding costs more than a right one gains.

Experiments:
Setting: ALFWorld valid_seen and valid_unseen (274 games), 2 seeds, identical seeds across arms; Qwen3-32B on two vLLM replicas (one per GPU, prefix caching on); k=3 (~430 tokens); a planted binding item replaces the same number of exemplar tokens so all arms are matched to +/-5 percent (verified from logged prefill counts). Bank manipulation: binding item = 'Scene signature <sorted receptacle inventory of the first observation>: <object class> was found in <receptacle>' (~30 tokens); planted from the game's PDDL for a random fraction r in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0} of games (assignment fixed per game across seeds and keys); natural level = same-floorplan train-split expert trajectories with their measured correctness rate p_nat (a fifth, uncontrolled rate); wrong level = plausible but incorrect receptacle for 100 percent of games. Query manipulation: goal key (goal sentence; BM25 and bge-m3 dense, index over trajectories plus binding items), initial-observation key (first observation text), stall key (current observation at the first repeated or 'Nothing happens' action, retrieved items appended as a new message so the cached prefix survives), random-within-type at matched length. Static arms: best type-conditioned static (3 fixed exemplars + procedure) and type-agnostic static (six procedures) from Measurement C, re-run; whole-binding-table static (type-agnostic prompt + all 274 rows, ~8k tokens, one cached prefix) at r=0.5 (137 true rows, the reader must find its row or ignore the table) and r=1.0. Positive control: oracle injection of the game's own binding. Cells: 3 keys x 4 rates (12) + random x {0, 1.0} (2) + wrong-binding x {goal, observation} (2) + whole-table x 2 + oracle (1) + natural x {goal, observation} (2) + 2 static re-runs = 23 cells, ~30 min each, ~6 h wall-clock with both GPUs saturated (episode workers tuned to the vLLM running queue >= 8 per replica). Order (pre-registered): static re-runs and oracle first (test-retest floor and positive control), then the rate ladder for the observation key, then the goal and stall keys, then wrong and natural levels. Cost: uncached prefill tokens per episode and cache hit rate per arm from vLLM logs; report accuracy vs uncached tokens. Ablations: scene signature as receptacle-inventory hash vs raw first-observation text; k=1 vs k=3 for the observation key at r=1.0; Qwen3-8B reader on the r=1.0 cells (exploratory). WebShop is out of scope for this claim (no scene structure); the bank statistic proposed for prediction is the fraction of items whose content varies within type conditional on the key.

Baselines and Ablations:
Baselines: best type-conditioned static arm and type-agnostic static arm (Measurement C, re-run on the same seeds); goal-keyed top-3 retrieval (Measurement A, re-run at r=0); random-within-type at matched length (separates the retriever from having type-relevant content); whole-binding-table cached static prompt (the whole-bank arm for the compact binding sub-bank); oracle binding injection (upper bound). Ablations: key encoder (BM25 vs bge-m3) for the goal and observation keys; scene-signature form; k=1 vs k=3 at r=1.0; stall key vs initial-observation key at r=1.0 (does timing of the state matter when the binding is scene-level); reader size (Qwen3-8B, exploratory); natural vs planted binding at the same measured correctness rate (realism check).

Falsifiable Predictions:
- P1 (rate-0 residual): at r=0 each key (goal, initial observation, stall) minus the best static arm lies within +/-8 net on the pooled 274x2 cell. Falsified if any key exceeds the best static arm by more than +8 net at r=0. Arm: the three r=0 retrieval cells against the re-run best static cell; nothing in the design prevents the object-specific exemplars they retrieve from adding success, which is what Measurement C's residual tests, so the outcome is open.
- P2 (key x rate interaction): the observation-key residual over the best static arm rises monotonically with r and reaches at least +13 net (half the Gate-0 gain) at r=1.0; the goal-key residual stays within +/-8 net at every r. Falsified if (a) the observation-key residual at r=1.0 is below +8 net (the key does not index the scene or the reader does not apply the binding), or (b) the goal-key residual at r=1.0 exceeds +8 net (object-class matching alone selects the right scene's row often enough). Arms: observation-key and goal-key cells at r=1.0 and the trend across r=0.25/0.5.
- P3 (whole table vs retrieval): at r=1.0 the whole-binding-table static arm (274 rows, cached) is within +/-8 net of observation-keyed retrieval, and at r=0.5 it is within +/-8 net of observation-keyed retrieval at r=0.5. Falsified if the table arm is below the retrieval arm by more than 8 net at either rate (the reader cannot locate its row among 274, or the 137 rows for other scenes at r=0.5 induce compliance). Arm: the two whole-table cells. Not entailed: the table contains the retrieved row, but whether an 8k-token table lies inside the reader's effective context is empirical and can come out either way.
- P4 (asymmetry): the wrong-binding arm with the observation key is below the r=0 observation-key cell by more than the r=1.0 observation-key cell is above it (harm exceeds gain). Falsified if harm <= gain, or if the wrong binding does not lower success by more than 8 net at all (the reader ignores bindings). Arm: wrong-binding observation-key cell paired with the r=0 and r=1.0 observation-key cells.
- P5 (random-within-type control): at r=1.0 random-within-type selection is within +/-8 net of the goal key and below the observation key by more than 8 net. Falsified if random matches the observation key (the binding is found by chance or the reader exploits something other than the key) or if random is below the goal key by more than 8 net (the goal key carries object-specific value that P1 missed). Arm: random-within-type cell at r=1.0.

Measurement and Noise Control:
Paired (game, seed) cells with identical seeds across arms; primary outcome task success; net = paired success difference in percentage points over 274x2 episodes; 95 percent CI from a cluster bootstrap over games (2,000 resamples); the +/-8-net floor is pre-registered and margins are stated as fractions of the Gate-0 retrieval gain (+27/+32 net); per-type numbers (+/-13-15) are exploratory. Test-retest: the best static arm is re-run with the same seeds to give the empirical per-cell noise. The rate ladder is tested by a paired linear trend over r with a cluster-bootstrap CI, not by eyeballing four cells. Binding assignment per game is fixed across seeds and keys so rate cells differ only in the bank; the same index code and k serve all keys; token matching within +/-5 percent and prefix-cache hit rate are verified from logs for every arm. Pre-registration of P1-P5 and the arm list before the first confirmatory cell; positive control (oracle injection) must clear +13 net over the best static arm before any null is interpreted.

### proposal: retrieval_timing_is_budgeting
Title: When to Retrieve Is Only a Budget Question: An Oracle-Decision Bound on the Value of Retrieval Timing for Experience Memory, With Failure-Keyed Retrieval at Stall Tested Against Planted Recoveries and a Compiled Rulebook
Short Hypothesis: The value of any retrieval-timing policy (start, on stall, fixed schedule, learned) over always-retrieve is bounded above by the mass of episodes on which retrieval fails but the cached static prompt succeeds. We measure that bound directly with an oracle-decision arm that retrieves exactly on the (game, seed) episodes the static prompt fails and predict it is inside the +/-8-net floor on ALFWorld, so stall-triggered retrieval can only re-trade uncached tokens for the same success. A failure-keyed query at stall beats a goal-keyed query only when the bank contains matching recovery segments (planted at 100 percent), not with the natural self pool, and a compiled recovery rulebook in the cached static prompt matches it.

Experiments:
Setting as in Measurement A/C: ALFWorld 274 games x 2 seeds, identical seeds across arms, Qwen3-32B on two vLLM replicas with prefix caching, k=3 goal-keyed retrieval (~430 tokens), best static arm from C. Re-runs: static (test-retest floor) and always-retrieve at start. Oracle-decision arm: per (game, seed), static if the static re-run succeeded, else retrieval at start; its difference from always-retrieve estimates P(static succeeds and retrieval fails) minus test-retest loss, read off the 2x2 paired contingency table. Stall trigger: first occurrence of (a) 'Nothing happens' twice in a row or (b) a repeated (observation, action) pair; thresholds 2 and 5 consecutive invalid actions; retrieved items appended as a new environment-style message; a fixed-step schedule arm retrieves at step 10 regardless. Query at stall: goal sentence; current observation; failure key (last three actions and observations). Banks: expert pool; self pool with detour windows indexed; planted-recovery bank (five ALFWorld failure modes: closed receptacle, wrong receptacle, object already held, wrong object instance, missing 'go to'; ~40-token recovery item keyed by failure text; 100 percent coverage); random-at-stall at matched length. Compiled control: recovery rulebook (the five items concatenated into the type-agnostic static prompt, ~200 tokens, cached), alone and with stall retrieval on top. Cells: static re-run, always re-run, oracle, fixed-step schedule, stall(threshold 2) x {goal, observation, failure} x {expert, self} (6), stall failure-key on planted bank, stall random, rulebook static, rulebook + planted stall retrieval, stall threshold 5 goal-key = 16 cells, ~30 min each, ~4 h with both GPUs busy. Logging: firing rate and step-at-fire for stall arms; uncached prefill tokens and cache hit rate per episode per arm; accuracy vs uncached tokens per episode as the cost axis. Order (pre-registered): re-runs and oracle first, then the planted positive control, then the self-pool and rulebook cells, then thresholds and schedule.

Baselines and Ablations:
Baselines: never-retrieve = best static arm (cached); always-retrieve at start (uncached, Measurement A re-run); fixed-step schedule (timing without a trigger); random-at-stall at matched length (content-free trigger); compiled recovery rulebook (static counterpart of failure-keyed retrieval); oracle-decision arm (upper bound on any timing policy). Ablations: stall threshold 2 vs 5; k=1 vs k=3 at stall (exploratory); self-pool indexing of whole trajectories vs detour windows only; failure key with vs without the current observation; rulebook with vs without stall retrieval on top.

Falsifiable Predictions:
- P1 (oracle bound): oracle-decision minus always-retrieve is within +/-8 net. Falsified if the oracle exceeds always-retrieve by 8 net or more, which occurs when retrieval fails on at least 8 percent of episodes the static prompt solves (look-at-in-light's -4.8 in Measurement A makes this producible). Arm: the oracle-decision cell against the always-retrieve re-run. Not entailed: on static-success episodes the oracle re-runs static and can lose test-retest cases while always-retrieve can succeed on those same episodes, so the difference can take either sign.
- P2 (stall as budgeting): stall-triggered goal-keyed retrieval (threshold 2, expert pool) is within +/-8 net of always-retrieve while firing on at most 50 percent of episodes. Falsified if it is below always-retrieve by more than 8 net (the stall arrives after the step budget is spent) or fires on more than 50 percent of episodes. Arm: the threshold-2 goal-key stall cell and its firing log. The fixed-step schedule cell adds a second test: if the schedule matches the stall trigger within +/-8 while firing on 100 percent of episodes, the trigger has no value beyond its firing rate, which is the claimed direction; the falsifier is the schedule falling below the stall trigger by more than 8 net.
- P3 (failure key needs matching content, positive control): failure-keyed retrieval from the planted-recovery bank at stall exceeds goal-keyed retrieval from the expert pool at stall by at least 8 net. Falsified if the difference is below 8 net (the failure text does not index the recovery, or the reader does not apply it at stall time). Arm: planted failure-key stall cell vs expert goal-key stall cell. If P3 fails, P4 is uninterpretable and is reported as such.
- P4 (natural self pool is decorative for the failure key): failure-keyed retrieval from the self pool at stall is within +/-8 net of goal-keyed retrieval from the expert pool at stall and of random-at-stall. Falsified if it exceeds either by more than 8 net (the agent's own detours are load-bearing content). Arm: self-pool failure-key stall cell against the expert goal-key stall cell and the random-at-stall cell.
- P5 (rulebook matches retrieval): the compiled recovery rulebook (cached) is within +/-8 net of planted failure-keyed retrieval at stall, and adding stall retrieval on top of the rulebook adds less than 8 net. Falsified if retrieval beats the rulebook by 8 net or more (recovery must be delivered at the moment of failure rather than read from the prefix), or if rulebook + retrieval exceeds rulebook by 8 net or more. Arm: rulebook cell and rulebook + planted stall retrieval cell.

Measurement and Noise Control:
Paired (game, seed) cells with identical seeds; primary outcome task success; net differences with cluster-bootstrap CIs over games; +/-8-net floor pre-registered, margins as fractions of the Gate-0 gain (+27/+32). The oracle bound is computed from the paired 2x2 table of (static success, retrieval success) per episode with a McNemar-style CI, and the static re-run gives the test-retest disagreement that the oracle arm inherits. Stall arms log firing rate and step-at-fire; cost is uncached prefill tokens per episode from vLLM logs, with cache hit rate verified for the static and rulebook arms. Positive controls (Measurement A's always-retrieve gain, P3's planted recovery) must clear the floor before P1, P2, P4 or P5 nulls are interpreted. Pre-registration of P1-P5 and arm order before the first confirmatory cell; per-type numbers exploratory.

### proposal: rank_flat_retriever_is_a_type_classifier
Title: Rank-Flat Retrieval: Within-Type Similarity Rank and Near-Duplicate Removal Do Not Move Task Success, So the Experience Retriever Reduces to a Type Classifier Plus a Similarity-Scaled Compliance Hazard
Short Hypothesis: On trajectory banks the retriever's contribution to task success is a step function of type match: replacing the top-3 items with items at within-type similarity rank 10-12 or 50-52, or removing every bank item that shares the test game's (type, object, receptacle) or (type, object), changes success by less than the +/-8-net floor, while removing the type drops success to the wrong-type placebo. On the harm side, the most similar wrong-type item hurts more than a random wrong-type item, and the harm is content-driven: relabelling the same items as retrieved memory, examples or instructions changes nothing, whereas an explicit mismatch warning reduces it. A per-type cached sub-bank of up to 80 items therefore matches top-3 retrieval at lower uncached cost.

Experiments:
Setting as in Measurement A/C: 274 games x 2 seeds, identical seeds across arms, Qwen3-32B on two vLLM replicas with prefix caching, k=3 (~430 tokens), items length-matched within +/-5 percent by choosing the nearest-length item inside each rank window. Rank ladder (type-conditioned retrieval, type read from the goal sentence as in Measurement A): ranks 1-3 (Measurement A re-run), 10-12, 50-52, random-within-type; a type-agnostic variant of the top-3 cell retrieves from the whole bank without the type filter. Leave-out ladder: per test game, remove bank items sharing (type, object class id, target receptacle id) [L1], (type, object class id) [L2], type [L3], then retrieve top-3 from the remainder; ALFWorld object class ids are used so synonyms do not leak. Harm arms: most-similar wrong-type items (nearest neighbours among other-type items by bge-m3 similarity to the goal) vs Measurement C's random wrong-type fixed exemplar (re-run); framing cells present the most-similar wrong-type items under 'Examples', 'Retrieved from memory of similar past tasks', 'Instructions: follow this procedure', and 'Examples (may be from a different task; follow only if the task matches)'. Whole-type sub-bank: all items of the game's type up to 80 (truncation rule: shortest first), one cached prefix per type. Static baselines: best type-conditioned static and type-agnostic static from Measurement C, re-run. Cells: rank ladder 4 + type-agnostic top-3 1 + leave-out 3 + harm 2 + framing 3 (the 'Examples' header is the similar-wrong cell) + sub-bank 1 + static re-runs 2 = 16 cells, ~30 min each, ~4 h with both GPUs busy. Secondary outcomes per cell: same-object recall, same-receptacle recall, mean similarity of returned items. Cost: uncached prefill tokens per episode per arm; the sub-bank arm's cache hit rate is verified. Order (pre-registered): static and A re-runs first (test-retest), then harm and warning arms (positive controls), then ladders and sub-bank. Per-type availability of rank-50 items is reported; for types with fewer than 55 items the deepest available rank window is used and declared.

Baselines and Ablations:
Baselines: best type-conditioned static arm and type-agnostic static arm (Measurement C re-runs); top-3 goal-keyed retrieval (Measurement A re-run); random-within-type at matched length; random wrong-type placebo (Measurement C, re-run). Ablations: type-conditioned vs type-agnostic top-3 (is the type filter doing the work); rank window 10-12 vs 50-52; leave-out L1 vs L2 vs L3; similar-wrong vs random-wrong; four framing headers; sub-bank at 40 vs 80 items (exploratory); BM25 vs bge-m3 for the rank ladder (exploratory).

Falsifiable Predictions:
- P1 (rank-flat): rank 50-52 within type is within +/-8 net of rank 1-3 on the pooled 274x2 cell. Falsified if rank 1-3 exceeds rank 50-52 by more than 8 net. Arm: the rank-50 cell against the rank-1 re-run. Not entailed: rank within a type varies object and receptacle match, which can carry success.
- P2 (near-duplicate independence): leave-out levels L1 and L2 each change success by less than 8 net relative to the full-bank top-3 cell. Falsified if either drops success by 8 net or more (the gain is carried by near-duplicates). Arm: L1 and L2 cells.
- P3 (type removal): L3 falls to within +/-8 net of the random wrong-type placebo. Falsified if L3 stays more than 8 net above the placebo (cross-type procedure transfers, e.g. heat to cool). Arm: L3 cell against the placebo re-run.
- P4 (similarity-scaled harm): the most-similar wrong-type arm is below the random wrong-type placebo by more than 8 net. Falsified if the difference is below 8 net (compliance does not scale with similarity). Arm: the similar-wrong ('Examples' header) cell against the placebo re-run.
- P5 (framing null with positive control): the 'Retrieved from memory' and 'Instructions' headers are within +/-8 net of the 'Examples' header, while the mismatch-warning header raises success by 8 net or more over 'Examples'. Falsified if either header shifts success by 8 net or more (framing drives compliance), or if the warning fails to shift it (then the null is uninterpretable and is reported as such). Arms: the three framing cells against the 'Examples' cell.
- P6 (per-type cached sub-bank): the whole-type sub-bank (up to 80 items, cached) is within +/-8 net of rank 1-3 with lower uncached prefill per episode. Falsified if it is below rank 1-3 by more than 8 net (many-shot degradation at ~11k tokens). Arm: the sub-bank cell. Not entailed: the sub-bank contains the top-3 items, but whether 80 trajectories inside the reader's context preserve their use is empirical.
- P7 (type filter is the retriever): type-agnostic top-3 retrieval from the whole bank is within +/-8 net of type-conditioned top-3. Falsified if the type-agnostic cell is below the type-conditioned cell by more than 8 net (similarity over the whole bank returns wrong-type items often enough to trigger P4's harm). Arm: the type-agnostic top-3 cell.

Measurement and Noise Control:
Paired (game, seed) cells with identical seeds; primary outcome task success; every retrieval cell is also reported as its difference from the best static arm so rank-flatness is anchored to the residual over static; net differences with cluster-bootstrap CIs over games (2,000 resamples); +/-8-net floor pre-registered, margins as fractions of the Gate-0 gain (+27/+32); per-type numbers exploratory (+/-13-15). Test-retest from the static and A re-runs. Secondary retriever metrics (same-object recall, mean similarity) are reported next to success for every cell so a flat success curve over a steep metric curve is visible. Token matching within +/-5 percent verified from logged prefill counts; item selection inside rank windows is length-matched, not similarity-matched. Positive controls (A's gain over static; P4's harm; P5's warning) must clear the floor before P1, P2 or P5 nulls are interpreted. Pre-registration of P1-P7 and arm order before the first confirmatory cell.
